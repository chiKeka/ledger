## How this works

- Every claim enters with a stated probability, the test that resolves it, a deadline, and provenance: **bruno** marks positions I have published or ratified, **twin** marks the system's own analysis. The two records are scored separately, and a number only ever scores to my record when I stated it myself (`confidence_source: bruno-stated`) — a twin-estimated probability on one of my claims scores to the twin, so the machine cannot launder its guesses into my track record.
- Probabilities are locked at registration. Resolution requires the stated test to be met; partial and unresolvable are honest outcomes, because stretched wins would corrupt the only thing this page is for. Every claim must be grounded in something that exists today - hypothetical constructs stay out, and a claim that slips through gets withdrawn in the open, never deleted.
- Brier score is the mean squared gap between stated probability and outcome: 0.000 is prophecy, 0.250 is coin-flipping. Skill is measured against three baselines and rendered in the record line above, each with a cluster-resampled 95% interval: skill vs coin-flip; skill vs base rate — the naive base rate recorded with each claim, with its provenance stated at registration; and skill vs market — the listed market price where one exists, the least flattering comparator, shown even when it is negative. A skill number whose interval spans zero is labeled not yet distinguishable from zero, because raw accuracy cannot distinguish judgment from easy questions.
- Correlated claims are disclosed, not hidden: linked claims (probes, sharpens, shared systems) collapse into clusters, the record line reports the effective number of independent events beside the raw count, and the intervals resample clusters, never single claims. Forecasts within 7% of their own base rate are echoes — they train calibration but cannot evidence judgment — so the record line reports the non-echo count separately and claims no skill on that subset below 30 resolutions.
- The evaluation is pre-registered (2026-07-18, `config.json#epistemic.evaluation_milestone`): demonstrated skill is claimed only at 100 scored claims (≥70 independent events, ≥20 worldview-frame, ≥10 structural, ≥30 non-echo) with the base-rate skill interval excluding zero. Until then, everything here is early evidence and says so.
- Forecasts are tagged by frame: **worldview** means the topic lives inside my analytical frameworks, **world** means it does not - market events, votes, dated milestones picked precisely because my frameworks have nothing to say about them. The calibration record is split by frame in the table under [Calibration record](#calibration-record), because being calibrated at home and being calibrated are different claims. A split too thin to read says so on the board instead of hiding.
- Claims can be linked: a fast **probe** takes a reading on a slow structural position, a **sharpens** link restates a vaguer claim measurably, an **opposes** link marks positions that cannot both fully hold, **shares-system** marks claims reading the same underlying system. Links are structure, never score - every claim is still resolved alone against its own locked probability.
- A resolution whose outcome was publicly knowable before the claim was registered is **invalidated**: it stays on the board forever with its reason, but it is excluded from the record, the Brier score, and calibration. The record does not keep wins it did not earn. Every new claim now requires a pre-registration evidence search confirming its test has not already fired.
- Methodology experiments — retrodictions run to test the ledger's own machinery against known history — are not forecasts about the world. They are kept off the live record entirely, in a separate workshop, so the instrument's testing never inflates or contaminates the score.
- A daily loop scans new signals against open claims and logs evidence; disconfirming evidence outranks news in my morning brief by design. A weekly loop settles accounts, and once enough resolutions accumulate, the system computes its own calibration corrections and applies them to future estimates.
- The machine-readable version of everything on this page is in [ledger.json](ledger.json).
